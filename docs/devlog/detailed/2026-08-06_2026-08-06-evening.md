## The fit was taught to read an underside panel, and nine minutes later proved it could not

**Timestamp:** 19:53 and 20:02

**Action:** Threaded a panel face through `fitStudLattice` and `solveAxonometricFromLattice` in `packages/rendering/src/camera-fit-lattice.ts` — 99 insertions against 8 — then measured the premise behind it and took the fitter's half back out, 67 against 21.

**Code reviewer comments:** No external reviewer; the author's own measurement refuted the commit nine minutes earlier. The first commit asserted as a regression test that "a below-view basis returns null when read as an above-view", and tied that to the eight panels of forty the camera fit already refused and to the `maxResidualFraction` docstring naming "a step drawn from underneath" as the case that lands over 0.03. Refitting all forty panels of the camera-fit run a second time as below-views produced no solution on any of them — including the five the flip icon and two blind raters agree are drawn from underneath — so the fit was not distinguishing the faces at all.

**Result:** `solveAxonometricFromLattice` had resolved the two roots of a projected square lattice by fiat, rejecting the negative one as "the same view mirrored, which upright art never prints" — right for a model that stays upright and wrong for 6651557, which is built partly inverted. The fix mirrors the measured basis so that a below-view becomes the above-view problem the solver already handles, measuring azimuth, scale and residual against the mirrored basis and negating the elevation on the way out; the default is studs-up, so all 18 existing tests and every existing caller are unchanged. The second commit then removed the option from the fitter rather than leaving it as an inert knob, because the reason it cannot work is exact rather than a tolerance: `a(A, -e) = a(-A, e)` and `b(A, -e) = -b(-A, e)`, and negating one basis vector spans the same lattice, so a below-view lattice at azimuth A *is* an above-view lattice at azimuth -A and the fitter's search over re-basings always reaches the positive-elevation twin.

**Reasoning:** No measurement of a stud grid can separate the two faces, which relocates the problem rather than solving it: the panel fit supplies azimuth, scale and phase, and the flip icon supplies the sign of the elevation a candidate is rendered at. Nothing about the fit needed to change; the caller did.

**Validation:** Four regressions in the first commit, and two in the second that make the lattice equivalence executable so the inert version cannot be added back. The negative elevation was checked separately by rendering one plate at +35 and -35 degrees rather than by argument: it does put the camera below, and the underside renders featureless because `builtin:plate-6x6` is a parametric box with no tubes — which does not reach the score, since the step score is silhouette-only (region IoU plus stroke recall and boundary precision).
