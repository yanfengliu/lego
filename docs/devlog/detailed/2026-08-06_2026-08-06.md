## Placement was reframed from an inference problem to a search the next panel scores

**Timestamp:** 09:53

**Action:** Landed owner direction into `docs/design/building-system.md` the session it was given — 26 lines, doc-only. Stop treating placement as inference (read this panel, output a transform) and treat it as a search the booklet itself scores.

**Code reviewer comments:** None from a reviewer; this is the owner's own direction, recorded as such.

**Result:** As inference the problem is underdetermined exactly where a rule cannot close it: the booklet gives placements rather than connections, about half its highlights are open contours, some steps are drawn exploded, and a panel difference finds the right stud without fixing the offset. As a search it is not underdetermined, because the booklet renders the same growing object 359 times — panel N+1 contains everything placed at step N, so a wrong placement produces a visibly wrong panel N+1 and every panel after. Propose, render from the panel's own camera, compare, and on disagreement undo and take the next candidate.

**Reasoning:** That dissolves the hard cases rather than solving them. An open contour stops mattering when the whole render is compared, and "the right stud but not the right offset" is a candidate set, which is what a search wants. Vision gets two jobs and the split is measured rather than stylistic: reading an exploded panel PROPOSES, cheaply, because a wrong candidate dies at the next panel; asking whether a render matches a printed panel CHECKS, and must be posed same-or-different, because the open N-way form measured 39.9% self-consistent on this booklet where the closed binary form scored 84 of 84 across two independent raters. Proposer output stays untrusted like every other provider result here. Symmetry defers the check rather than defeating it — a quarter-turned 2x2 plate is indistinguishable alone, but the booklet keeps building on top of every placement, so the mistake surfaces when something breaks the symmetry. The requirement is therefore not resolving ambiguity before committing but going back far enough when it surfaces: deep backtracking, and a history the search can rewind, which yields a measurable (steps undone, deepest reversal) that can be driven down where "prove this placement unique" cannot.

**Validation:** Doc-only. It names what already exists to serve it rather than proposing new machinery: `compareBuilds` scores a rebuild per step, `capture_model_views` renders canonical views, the camera is solved because a panel's own stud grid fits the booklet's angle and scale with no part identities, and lattice, connectors, collision and the depletion walk prune before any render.

**Notes:** One claim in it measured false the next day on this very booklet — "an exploded step need not be read at all, because the next one is not exploded". Steps 1 and 2 of set 6651557 are both exploded and step 3 is the first seated panel; recorded in `2026-08-06_2026-08-07.md`.

## The search was allowed to be wrong and come back, and told to count how far back it went

**Timestamp:** 10:43

**Action:** Added `runBacktrackingSearch` — new `apps/web/src/assembly/backtracking-search.ts` (233 lines) with a 230-line suite, exported from `assembly/index.ts` — and lifted the enumerate/prune/render/score step out of `advanceBeam` into a shared `expandStep` so the beam and the backtracker cannot drift.

**Code reviewer comments:** None recorded at this commit. A critic showed 48 minutes later that the headline counter read zero on exactly the run where it is the answer; see below.

**Result:** `runBuildSearch` stopped at the first step that killed the beam, so an ambiguous step was a dead end. That is the wrong requirement — a 2x2 plate rotated a quarter turn renders identically to the correct one, and the step it is made on holds no evidence against it. Refusing such a step is what a build cannot afford, because the step after a refusal has nothing to attach to. The driver now commits to the best candidate, keeps every rejected alternative, and on failure walks back to the shallowest step with an untried one. Nothing is deleted: `BuildTree` addresses a node by its parent and its placement, so returning to an abandoned branch finds the work already there and the wrong branch survives as the counterevidence it is.

**Reasoning:** The booklet does not need a step to settle itself. It draws the same growing object 359 times, so a symmetric mistake stops being symmetric as soon as something lands that breaks the symmetry. What that demands is not certainty up front but the ability to go back far enough when the contradiction finally arrives.

**Validation:** It reports the numbers the plan asked for — reversals, how far back the deepest one reached, and every step undone — plus the candidates the render budget never reached, so a completed build cannot quietly mean a build that only looked at some of them, and it stops on an exhausted budget rather than returning whichever prefix it was holding.

## The loop would have scored against the wrong face of the model

**Timestamp:** 18:28

**Action:** Recorded the finding in `docs/design/building-system.md` (8 lines, placed above the `3020;L` frame in the order), a full section in `docs/learning/lessons-evidence.md`, and one line in `docs/learning/lessons.md`.

**Code reviewer comments:** No code reviewer — the owner found it by eye in seconds from a rendered page, identifying steps 4, 7, 10 and 11 as back views, after the pipeline had read XML and source instead.

**Result:** Set 6651557 is built partly upside down. The booklet turns it over mid-build and prints an icon at each turn — a white 44.937pt rounded square holding a curved arrow around a sphere — and over printed steps 1 to 12 that icon is a strict toggle of which side the panel is drawn from: 1-3 studs up, 4 underside, 5 studs up, 6 studs up, 7 underside, 8 studs up, 9 studs up, 10 underside, 11 still underside. Read off pages 11 to 15 by eye, every one checks, and all four of the owner's back views fall out of the toggle. From step 4 onward a candidate render would be compared against the opposite face of the printed drawing — which puts this **above** the `3020;L` frame in the order, because clearing that frame would have started the placement loop and then failed it from step 4 for a reason nothing in the run reports.

**Reasoning:** The pipeline had already half-found it. `deriveTransitionPanelFeatures` detects the icon exactly; an earlier pass measured it across all 224 pages and concluded it annotates the viewpoint rather than naming the action, then correctly refused to map it to the `rotation` action class because 33 of the 39 it found also place pieces and reading it as an action would mislabel six placement steps. That refusal was right. What never happened is anything consuming it: no viewpoint state exists, `transition-classifications.json` holds 25 attachments and one final-view with zero rotations and nothing before step 44, and the note "that gap is what a vision call is for" was left as a note. The lesson landed is that shape — refusing to use a cue for the wrong purpose is not the same as using it for the right one, and "this signal means X, but X is not what I was asking" is the moment to write down who consumes X.

**Validation:** Two measurement errors are named as what kept it invisible. The icon count is an undercount: attribution requires the icon's centre to fall inside `panel.bounds` and step 8's sits above its artwork, so page 13 prints two icons and only step 7's is seen — the recorded "39 icons, one per page" is that assumption written down as a measurement. And the camera fit cannot contradict any of it, because a projected square lattice reads the same from above or below; it reports a positive elevation on all 32 fitted panels including the flipped ones, median +35.59 degrees, which its own legend already calls a fit quality and not a proof.
