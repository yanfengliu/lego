## The official model's world placements were mirrored

**Timestamp:** 2026-08-07

**Action:** `resolveBuilderBoneTransform` read a Builder Bone through `diag(1,-1,1)` — determinant −1, a reflection. The correct LXFML-to-LDraw change of basis is `diag(1,-1,-1)`, a 180° rotation about x. The sign vector is shared between `transformedMatrix` and the position mapping, so both halves had to move together; they are now one named constant.

**Code reviewer comments:** The confirmation agent refuted the prescription I had recorded. Negating the Bone position's z **alone** makes the model worse, not better: an interlock census scored the incumbent at 64 stud∩stud and 65 clutch∩clutch impossible coincidences, the position-only change at 48 and 48 with the largest body-overlap volume of any reading, and the full `diag(1,-1,-1)` at **0 and 0** with the most interlock of all. Changing only the position turns a reflection into a map that is not rigid at all. A later critic then caught that the fix left `80015;E` disagreeing with the `.ldr` export and had re-pinned a golden from that frame — which turned out to be the export's error, not the pipeline's, but the flag was correct to raise.

**Result:** Every world placement in the 1465-brick model was mirrored. A rotation-only cross-check that never touches a position: one constant per-design frame explains **1129 of 1129** instances under the corrected basis against **443** under the old one.

**Reasoning:** The Shell decode negating all three axes is correct — the Android asset bundle is left-handed, pinned by the chiral design `54383;F`, a *right* wedge plate, at p95 1.250 LDU as decoded against 10.714 with z flipped. That handedness was then applied to right-handed LXFML Bone data. The local part frames were right and only the world placements were mirrored, which is why nothing caught it: every check this repository had compared a part to **its own surface**, and a mirror leaves that exactly as correct as before. Eight admitted designs passed that way. Step 1's own two parts cannot settle it either — both are mirror-ambiguous under a free upright fit, within 0.03 LDU either way.

**Validation:** The editor, which refuses a placement nothing holds up at the command, refused step 1's second piece under the old transforms — "would rest 8 LDU above the build plate with nothing under it" — and places both under the corrected ones, deriving three stud/clutch pairs. The repository already declared the right frame in `scripts/builder_ldraw_frame.py` and `part-model.md`; nothing compared the two.

**Notes:** `canonicalTransform` is what a placed piece is graded against, so every retained run bundle predating this was scored against the mirrored target and is kept only as history.

**Validation:** Six harmless false positives made the one real outlier unbelievable, so it was attributed to a systematic pipeline error rather than one brick. That is the cost recorded in the new lesson.

**Reasoning:** Panel N+1 draws everything placed at step N as already-built, seated and unhighlighted. That makes it an independent witness the step's own panel frequently cannot be. It is also simply how a person reads these instructions.

**Validation:** An independent probe that never ran the loop identified the same branch and recorded the same winning score to **seventeen significant figures**.

**Notes:** The four candidates are one model at four quarter turns, so this fixes the camera's quarter-turn coset rather than the absolute orientation; the completion audit will read step 1's transforms as rotated against the official model — a global world-frame rotation, not a wrong placement.
