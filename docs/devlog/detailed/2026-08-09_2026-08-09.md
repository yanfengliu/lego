# Devlog — 2026-08-09

One session, one unit of change: teach the connector backing policy what a clutch is, then prove it by drawing the first shell.

The session's shape is that almost every step was a no-op that had to be *proved* a no-op. Widening an admission rule and bumping a catalog version both look like they could move anything, and the only way to hand over "nothing else changed" as a fact rather than a hope is to dump the whole catalog before and after and diff it.

## `faceHoldsStud` was answering the opposite question on the underside, and it always had

`connector-backing-policy.ts` admitted a connector through one predicate, documented as *whether a whole stud's worth of the named face is backed by solid*. It samples a 5×5 grid over the stud footprint and requires some body box reaching that face to contain every sample. Four callers depend on it: the grid stud path and the grid clutch path, which silently skip a cell that fails, and the explicit `studOffsetsLdu` and `clutchOffsetsLdu` paths, which throw.

That is correct for a stud. A stud is material standing proud of the body, so it needs body behind it to stand on. Put to an underside it is not merely imprecise, it is inverted: it says a filled block accepts a clutch, when a filled block is the one thing that cannot, because the incoming stud has nowhere to go.

The reason it never fired is that no part modelled a cavity. Every plate and brick is one filled prism, `bodyBoxesLdu` is `undefined`, and the predicate returns `true` unconditionally. The bug was invisible for exactly as long as no part told the truth about its own underside.

What matters for the next session: this is why shells could not simply be emitted. A shell's bottom face stops being solid precisely where the clutches are, so `faceHoldsStud("bottom", …)` fails there and all 74 parts' clutches would have been refused at once — which would have read as the shells being wrong.

## The clutch rule, and why its grip range is zero rather than a tolerance

`cavityHoldsStud` sits beside `faceHoldsStud` and answers the clutch question from the body union. Three conditions:

- **clearance** — no body box crosses the cylinder the incoming stud sweeps, which is exactly the volume the `tubeSeat` allowance already reserves for it;
- **grip** — some box standing in that band reaches the stud's own `STUD_RADIUS_LDU` circle without crossing it;
- **seat** — the cavity is roofed over the whole footprint, so the stud bottoms out rather than passing through the part.

The grip range is zero because `3020.dat` makes it zero. Line 21's cavity `box5` has half-extent 16 on the axis that is catalog *x*; the clutch centres sit on the 10 LDU half-pitch; the stud radius is exactly 6. `16 - 10 = 6` to the LDU. A clutch *is* an interference fit, so the wall touches the stud and any looser number would have been invented. This is the one place a tolerance would have been easy to add and wrong to add.

Admission is `cavityHoldsStud(x, z).held || faceHoldsStud("bottom", x, z)` — a strict widening. The fallback is not an exemption and is documented as terminal: it exists only for parts that model no cavity at all, which are exactly the parts `body-is-hollow-where-it-clutches` reports, and it dies with the last of them.

**Proved a no-op, not assumed.** A probe dumped every part's connectors, allowances, collision primitives, geometry modes and bounds to JSON before and after. With the policy change alone, the two files were byte-identical across all 85 parts and both `catalog.hash` and the full truth hash were unmoved. The same probe is what later showed the shell touching exactly one part.

## `undersideMode` is now derived from the geometry, and it was spelled out twice

The mode was computed independently in `part-factory.ts` (for the definition) and in `makeGeometryDigestInput` (for the hash). Two copies of one rule, and the hash is the thing that binds the claim — so a drift between them would have bound the wrong claim silently. Both now read `undersideModeFor`.

More importantly the input changed. `buildConnectorFeatures` reports `undersideIsModelled`: true only when the part has clutches and *every* one of them was admitted through a cavity. So `modelled-shell-cavity` is a measurement of the body union rather than a declaration about it. Fill the cavity back in and the mode reverts to `semantic-tube-seat-grid` on its own — there is a test for exactly that, because a claim that can outrun its geometry is the defect this whole plan exists to remove.

A part with mixed clutches reports false. Half a modelled underside is still an underside a from-below comparison would measure wrongly.

## The tube's outer radius was measured all along — the missing file was not the missing method

`part-geometry-plan.md` recorded, honestly, that the tube's outer radius could not be measured because `stud4.dat` is absent from `scripts/ldraw-cache/`. That was true about `stud4.dat` and false about the cache.

`1-4stud4.dat` — "Stud Tube Open 0.25" — is in the cache and carries the whole profile. Line 13 places `1-4cylo.dat` scaled 6 (the bore, agreeing with `stud4od.dat`), line 14 places `1-4cylo.dat` scaled **8** (the outer wall), and line 15's `1-4ring3.dat` scaled 2 caps the annulus between 6 and 8, corroborating both. `2-4stud4.dat` lines 19–20 repeat it. The tube is 4 LDU tall, exactly the cavity depth.

The consequence is a small surprise worth keeping: the clutch's interference fit is at the **wall**, not at the tube. A clutch centre on the 10 LDU half-pitch is `10√2 = 14.142` LDU from the nearest tube centre against `6 + 8 = 14`, so LDraw's idealised tube clears the stud by 0.142 LDU while the cavity wall touches it exactly.

This is the "a blocker you inherited is a claim, not a fact" lesson landing again, in the mildest possible form: the absence was recorded correctly and then believed for two sessions without anyone re-searching the directory.

## The tubes are still absent, and now for a reason rather than for want of a number

`bodyBoxesLdu` takes boxes. A tube is an annulus with a 6 LDU bore. Drawing it as a solid box or a solid cylinder would fill that bore and replace one lie with another — the same class of error the plan exists to close. Every clutch on `plate-2x4` is held by the walls alone, so the first shell does not need them.

What that defers: the tubes' actual contribution is the *opposing* grip at an interior seat. A 1×N part's stud is gripped by two facing walls; a 2×N part's interior seat is gripped by a wall on one side and a tube on the other. The rule as written requires only one contact, and the comment says why — requiring opposed contact would refuse a clutch that is real, because of geometry deliberately left out. Generalising to the 74 will reach a part whose interior seats have no wall within reach, and the union has to be able to hold a hollow cylinder before that.

## The shell, and what `parts:check` did

`plate-2x4` carries five boxes: a ceiling slab spanning the full footprint from y −4 to 0, and four perimeter walls 4 LDU thick standing the cavity's 4 LDU. Every number traces to `3020.dat` line 21 or line 30 or to subtraction between them. Axes are swapped against LDraw (catalog *x* is the two-stud width) and catalog *y* is LDraw *y* − 4.

`parts:check`: **139 → 137**. `underside-is-drawn` 74 → 73, `body-is-hollow-where-it-clutches` 57 → 56, `geometry-mode-is-declared` 8 → 8. Exactly one part left exactly two rules. The catalog diff shows `builtin:plate-2x4` changing three fields — `primitives` 9 → 13, `bodyMode`, `undersideMode` — and `connectors` and `allowances` are *not* among them.

Independent corroboration arrived from a direction nobody aimed at it. The catalog version bump invalidates the 15 reviewed Builder source pins in `real-build-builder-sources.ts`, each carrying four digests. Regenerating them moved **15 definition digests** (every part's provenance carries the catalog version), **1 geometry digest**, **1 collision digest** — and **zero connector digests**, including `plate-2x4`'s. The part's connector list is byte-identical to what it was before it had a cavity.

## The pictures, which is the half that cannot be argued

The canonical straight-on `underside` view is nearly useless here and it is worth knowing why: the wall bottoms and the recessed ceiling share a downward normal, so under the flat finish they take identical lighting and the cavity shows only as a faint inset seam. A first look at that image would support either conclusion.

A three-quarter view from below settles it immediately. Middle-drag rotates in this app (`LEFT: null, MIDDLE: ROTATE, RIGHT: PAN`), and dragging *up* by about 150–215 px swings the camera under the model; dragging down goes over the top and clamps at the pole, which is what the first attempt did and it produced a picture of the studs.

Posed as a closed same-or-different question against `plate-2x3`, still a filled prism, at the same viewpoint: the 2×4 is a **tray** — a bright rim of four wall bottoms with visible thickness on every side, the ceiling recessed inside it, the inner faces of the near walls lit as separate strips. The 2×3 is a flat dark rectangle running edge to edge with no rim and no recess. Different, in the direction claimed.

## Three baselines moved, none of them because of a placement

`--last-step` 5, 4 and 3 report 5/8, 4/6 and 3/4 exactly as recorded, with every placed transform unchanged. All three structural hashes moved.

`structuralDocumentValue` includes `truth: normalized.truth`, so a catalog version bump re-hashes every document that pins it, whatever its parts do. The claim is not left as an argument: restoring `truth.catalog` to `builtin.basic-parts/8` / `sha256:a9adf38b…` and `truth.collisionModel` to `sha256:8f181d6f…` in each retained `document.json`, and changing nothing else, reproduces `sha256:f15eadfa…`, `sha256:64017a83…` and `sha256:6d46478c…` bit for bit.

The same technique saved a test rather than weakening it. `mesh-assets.test.ts` pinned the 77 legacy geometry hashes to a literal that had held since `/6`, proving each admission appended parts instead of regenerating them. `plate-2x4` is row 13, so that literal was going to move for the first time. Putting *only* that row back to its `/8` hash recovers the `/6` literal exactly — which is a stronger statement than the original, because it says one row moved and names it.

`real-build.spec.ts` still exits 1, on the pre-existing world-frame completion audit and `visual-evidence-unverified`. Every refusal is `run-incomplete` on a mirrored transform or the missing Node-side visual audit; none is geometric.

## Two tests changed shape, and the reason is worth inheriting

Both were true statements that stopped being true because one part is now honest and its neighbours are not.

`mass-properties` measured a plate as "its box plus its studs". It is a shell now: 25600 − 9216 + studs, and the 9216 is the cavity to the LDU. That test got stronger.

"A wedge has less material than the plate that contains it" **failed**, correctly: `wedge-plate-2x4-left` is still a filled prism at 21009.6 against the hollow plate's 20003.1. The property under test is the cut removing material, so the yardstick moved to the solid prism the wedge is cut from and is named in the test rather than read off a part of a different modelling generation. It goes back to comparing the two parts when the wedge is shelled too. This will recur: every cross-part comparison in this repository silently assumes both sides are modelled at the same fidelity, and shelling the catalog one part at a time breaks that assumption one comparison at a time.

## Prove a new test fails before trusting it

`connector-backing-policy.test.ts` was checked by mutating `cavityHoldsStud` to always refuse. Four of its six cases went red; the other two — "the body fills its own cavity" and "still demands solid behind a stud" — passed both ways *by design*, because they are the did-not-weaken guards and would fail under the opposite mutation. Worth stating so a later reader does not mistake them for dead tests.
