# Devlog — 2026-08-09

One session, one unit of change: teach the connector backing policy what a clutch is, then prove it by drawing the first shell.

The session's shape is that almost every step was a no-op that had to be *proved* a no-op. Widening an admission rule and bumping a catalog version both look like they could move anything, and the only way to hand over "nothing else changed" as a fact rather than a hope is to dump the whole catalog before and after and diff it.

## `faceHoldsStud` was answering the opposite question on the underside, and it always had

`connector-backing-policy.ts` admitted a connector through one predicate, documented as *whether a whole stud's worth of the named face is backed by solid*. It samples a 5×5 grid over the stud footprint and requires some body box reaching that face to contain every sample. Four callers depend on it: the grid stud path and the grid clutch path, which silently skip a cell that fails, and the explicit `studOffsetsLdu` and `clutchOffsetsLdu` paths, which throw.

That is correct for a stud. A stud is material standing proud of the body, so it needs body behind it to stand on. Put to an underside it is not merely imprecise, it is inverted: it says a filled block accepts a clutch, when a filled block is the one thing that cannot, because the incoming stud has nowhere to go.

The reason it never fired is that no part modelled a cavity. Every plate and brick is one filled prism, `bodyBoxesLdu` is `undefined`, and the predicate returns `true` unconditionally. The bug was invisible for exactly as long as no part told the truth about its own underside.

What matters for the next session: this is why shells could not simply be emitted. A shell's bottom face stops being solid precisely where the clutches are, so `faceHoldsStud("bottom", …)` fails there and all 74 parts' clutches would have been refused at once — which would have read as the shells being wrong.

## The clutch rule, and why its grip range is zero rather than a tolerance

`cavityHoldsStud` sits beside `faceHoldsStud` and answers the clutch question from the body union. Three conditions:

- **clearance** — no body box crosses the cylinder the incoming stud sweeps, which is exactly the volume the `tubeSeat` allowance already reserves for it;
- **grip** — some box standing in that band reaches the stud's own `STUD_RADIUS_LDU` circle without crossing it;
- **seat** — the cavity is roofed over the whole footprint, so the stud bottoms out rather than passing through the part.

The grip range is zero because `3020.dat` makes it zero. Line 21's cavity `box5` has half-extent 16 on the axis that is catalog *x*; the clutch centres sit on the 10 LDU half-pitch; the stud radius is exactly 6. `16 - 10 = 6` to the LDU. A clutch *is* an interference fit, so the wall touches the stud and any looser number would have been invented. This is the one place a tolerance would have been easy to add and wrong to add.

Admission is `cavityHoldsStud(x, z).held || faceHoldsStud("bottom", x, z)` — a strict widening. The fallback is not an exemption and is documented as terminal: it exists only for parts that model no cavity at all, which are exactly the parts `body-is-hollow-where-it-clutches` reports, and it dies with the last of them.

**Proved a no-op, not assumed.** A probe dumped every part's connectors, allowances, collision primitives, geometry modes and bounds to JSON before and after. With the policy change alone, the two files were byte-identical across all 85 parts and both `catalog.hash` and the full truth hash were unmoved. The same probe is what later showed the shell touching exactly one part.

## `undersideMode` is now derived from the geometry, and it was spelled out twice

The mode was computed independently in `part-factory.ts` (for the definition) and in `makeGeometryDigestInput` (for the hash). Two copies of one rule, and the hash is the thing that binds the claim — so a drift between them would have bound the wrong claim silently. Both now read `undersideModeFor`.

More importantly the input changed. `buildConnectorFeatures` reports `undersideIsModelled`: true only when the part has clutches and *every* one of them was admitted through a cavity. So `modelled-shell-cavity` is a measurement of the body union rather than a declaration about it. Fill the cavity back in and the mode reverts to `semantic-tube-seat-grid` on its own — there is a test for exactly that, because a claim that can outrun its geometry is the defect this whole plan exists to remove.

A part with mixed clutches reports false. Half a modelled underside is still an underside a from-below comparison would measure wrongly.

## The tube's outer radius was measured all along — the missing file was not the missing method

`part-geometry-plan.md` recorded, honestly, that the tube's outer radius could not be measured because `stud4.dat` is absent from `scripts/ldraw-cache/`. That was true about `stud4.dat` and false about the cache.

`1-4stud4.dat` — "Stud Tube Open 0.25" — is in the cache and carries the whole profile. Line 13 places `1-4cylo.dat` scaled 6 (the bore, agreeing with `stud4od.dat`), line 14 places `1-4cylo.dat` scaled **8** (the outer wall), and line 15's `1-4ring3.dat` scaled 2 caps the annulus between 6 and 8, corroborating both. `2-4stud4.dat` lines 19–20 repeat it. The tube is 4 LDU tall, exactly the cavity depth.

The consequence is a small surprise worth keeping: the clutch's interference fit is at the **wall**, not at the tube. A clutch centre on the 10 LDU half-pitch is `10√2 = 14.142` LDU from the nearest tube centre against `6 + 8 = 14`, so LDraw's idealised tube clears the stud by 0.142 LDU while the cavity wall touches it exactly.

This is the "a blocker you inherited is a claim, not a fact" lesson landing again, in the mildest possible form: the absence was recorded correctly and then believed for two sessions without anyone re-searching the directory.

## The tubes are still absent, and now for a reason rather than for want of a number

`bodyBoxesLdu` takes boxes. A tube is an annulus with a 6 LDU bore. Drawing it as a solid box or a solid cylinder would fill that bore and replace one lie with another — the same class of error the plan exists to close. Every clutch on `plate-2x4` is held by the walls alone, so the first shell does not need them.

What that defers: the tubes' actual contribution is the *opposing* grip at an interior seat. A 1×N part's stud is gripped by two facing walls; a 2×N part's interior seat is gripped by a wall on one side and a tube on the other. The rule as written requires only one contact, and the comment says why — requiring opposed contact would refuse a clutch that is real, because of geometry deliberately left out. Generalising to the 74 will reach a part whose interior seats have no wall within reach, and the union has to be able to hold a hollow cylinder before that.

## Two tests changed shape, and the reason is worth inheriting

Both were true statements that stopped being true because one part is now honest and its neighbours are not.

`mass-properties` measured a plate as "its box plus its studs". It is a shell now: 25600 − 9216 + studs, and the 9216 is the cavity to the LDU. That test got stronger.

"A wedge has less material than the plate that contains it" **failed**, correctly: `wedge-plate-2x4-left` is still a filled prism at 21009.6 against the hollow plate's 20003.1. The property under test is the cut removing material, so the yardstick moved to the solid prism the wedge is cut from and is named in the test rather than read off a part of a different modelling generation. It goes back to comparing the two parts when the wedge is shelled too. This will recur: every cross-part comparison in this repository silently assumes both sides are modelled at the same fidelity, and shelling the catalog one part at a time breaks that assumption one comparison at a time.

## Prove a new test fails before trusting it

`connector-backing-policy.test.ts` was checked by mutating `cavityHoldsStud` to always refuse. Four of its six cases went red; the other two — "the body fills its own cavity" and "still demands solid behind a stud" — passed both ways *by design*, because they are the did-not-weaken guards and would fail under the opposite mutation. ## Generalising the shell to fifty-eight parts, and the three defects that only the picture and a two-brick stack could find

**Timestamp:** 2026-08-09, the session after `1e31eb9`, which inherited step 3 half-written and unverified in the working tree.

**Action:** Finished `part-shell.ts`, fixed what it broke, and looked at the result. The derivation is a pure function of the part's own footprint — erode it by the 4 LDU wall, roof it with the 4 LDU ceiling, stand a tube at the centre of every complete 2 x 2 block of stud cells — with a family whose LDraw file nobody has read made to throw rather than inherit a plate's numbers.

**Result:** `parts:check` **137 → 24**. `body-is-hollow-where-it-clutches` **56 → 0**, `underside-is-drawn` **73 → 16**, `geometry-mode-is-declared` 8 → 8. Catalog `/9 → /10`; 58 of 85 parts shelled. Booklet prefix unchanged at 5/5 steps and 8 pieces (`--last-step 5`) and 4/4 and 6 (`--last-step 4`).

**Three defects, each in a place a passing test could not see:**

*The tube's collision primitive cannot be a cylinder.* `collisions.ts` converts a body cylinder to its bounding box, and says why in its own comment: it over-claims, so it refuses what a real wheel would allow and never the reverse. A wheel stands alone. A tube sits at the centre of a 2 x 2 block with four studs at its corners, 10 * sqrt(2) = 14.142 LDU from the axis against a true 8 + 6 = 14. The box reaches 8 along each axis, so its nearest point to a stud centre is 2.83 LDU — inside the stud's own 6. Every exactly seated stack of two 2-wide parts reported `PART_STUD_BODY_COLLISION` against its own tubes, *with the connection declared*. It is now the largest axis-aligned box inside the tube circle, half-side 8/sqrt(2): its corners lie exactly on that circle in the four diagonal directions the studs occupy, so it reports the tube's own 0.142 LDU clearance, and it under-reaches only along the two axes, where the next tube 20 LDU away closes the gap.

*The tube's 144 triangles were wound backwards.* Every material here is `FrontSide`, so the GPU culled all of them: three tube meshes in the scene for a 2x4 plate, `deriveBrickScene` returning them, a unit test counting seven body meshes and passing, and a from-below capture that was a flat red rectangle — the picture the shell exists to replace, reproduced by the geometry added to replace it.

*A shell's box decomposition is not canonical under rotation.* `mergeLayer` sweeps x before z, so a square plate's wall ring comes out as two boxes running the full length and two inset between them. The union is unchanged by a quarter turn; the set of boxes is not. The Builder frame self-symmetry proof compared the boxes, so it called `plate-8x8`, `plate-4x4`, `plate-2x2`, every 1x1 and the 2x2 jumper asymmetric and sent them to the surface witness — losing the property the module exists for. The proof now compares the volume the boxes occupy, cut onto one shared grid so the comparison stays exact.

**Reasoning:** The decomposition could have been made canonical instead of the proof. It was not, for two reasons. A pinwheel is only well defined on a rectangular ring, and the general footprint here is an L; and step 4 will re-derive the eight mesh-first bodies into boxes nobody chooses by hand, which will not be canonical either. Comparing the volume is the question the proof means to ask, and it is the one that survives step 4.

**Validation:** The seated-stack case in `validation.test.ts` and the winding measurement in `rendering.test.ts` both fail on the pre-fix code — the second was written from the measurement that found the defect, 48 triangles pointing up into the part and none down. The tube approximation was swept exhaustively rather than argued: a stud at all 9,801 integer positions under a 2x4 plate, a 4x4 plate and a 2x4 brick — the schema admits only integer LDU positions, so that is every position, not a sample — admitting none whose drawn annulus overlaps it. The pictures were looked at, orbited under the model rather than captured straight down: 2x4 plate a tray with three tubes on its centre line, 4x4 plate the 3 x 3 grid `3031.dat` places, 2x4 brick a 20 LDU cavity with three full-height tubes, 1x4 plate a tray with no tubes at all.

**What the proof still misses:** the straight-down orthographic `underside` capture cannot see any of this, because the wall bottoms, the tube rings and the recessed ceiling are all faces with one normal and one material. Nothing automated looks at the cavity yet; a from-below silhouette check against the expanded LDraw surface is still owed, and it is what would have caught the winding without a human eye.

**Action:** `mesh-underside.ts` reads the bundled surface at each declared clutch and reports the lowest horizontal body surface standing over it. `measured-part-factory.ts` uses that to set `undersideMode`, alongside a constant `bodyMode` and a `studMode` taken from whether the expansion carried stud seats.

**Result:** `parts:check` 24 to 16; `geometry-mode-is-declared` 8 to 0. Catalog /10 to /11. Of the fifteen reviewed Builder design pins, fourteen moved only their definition digest and one also moved its geometry digest, which is the single mesh part among them.

**Reasoning:** The obvious reading of the plan's step 4 — "re-derive the 8 mesh-first parts through the same path" — means making them parametric, and the owner decision of 2026-08-04 forbids exactly that: generated approximations were rejected because lost curve fidelity fails the measurement the goal is judged by. These parts already draw the real expanded LDraw surface. The gap was never fidelity; it was that the recipe named no mode, so the standard had nothing to check. A hand-written mode would have been the very thing the standard exists to catch, so the mode is measured.

**Validation:** Five cases in `mesh-underside.test.ts`, three of which are synthetic geometry rather than shipped parts: a slab with one flat bottom face reports `flat` and `false`, the same slab cut into a shell reports `recessed` and `true`, and a clutch over the hole reports `open`. The failing case exists because the passing one alone would not distinguish a measurement from an assertion.

**What the proof still misses:** nothing yet compares a mesh part's from-below render against a printed panel, which is the measurement all of this is for.

## Two claims in the plan turned out to be wrong, and both were worth checking rather than inheriting

The plan said the wing plate's 115 collision boxes were "a voxelised solid being drawn literally", and used that as the reason to replace them. They are not drawn: `createCatalogPartGeometry` branches on `generatorId` and takes the mesh path for all eight measured parts, so their collision primitives never reach the renderer. The claim was made from reading the seam rather than the branch.

What is actually wrong there is the same defect the shell fixed on the other side, one layer over: a per-column height field is filled top to bottom, so collision fills a cavity the mesh draws hollow. That is worth fixing and was not fixed here — it means regenerating pinned generated tables through `emit-measured-part-tables.py`, whose scorer refuses to write anything unless every part passes. What was established is that it is feasible: the meshes' winding is perfectly consistent, every triangle at the top face pointing up and every one at the bottom face pointing down across all eight parts with no exceptions, so a ceiling can be told from a floor and a column's solid runs derived without the crossing-parity test an open LDraw surface defeats.

The second claim was mine to make and I checked it before making it. The seven wedge and arc plates among the remaining sixteen have exactly the plate's 4 LDU cavity, so the temptation is to run the same erosion over them. The two arches do not: `3659`'s ceiling measures 768 LDU squared where eroding its 20 x 80 plan by 4 predicts 864, and 768 is 12 x 64, so its end walls are 8 LDU. `3455` says the same, 1248 against 1344, and 1248 is 12 x 104. Two files, one number, and it is not the plate's. Shelling them on the plate rule would have been a shell built from plausible numbers, which is the error this plan opens by naming.

## Where the sixteen actually stand

Every one of them was expanded from the pinned official archive and its underside measured, and the table is in `part-geometry-plan.md` so the next attempt starts from numbers rather than from a fetch. The blocker for the seven wedge and arc plates is representation and not measurement: the wall ring a shell needs is the plan minus the plan eroded by 4, which is not a wedge, not an arc and not a box union. It decomposes exactly into convex quad prisms, one per plan edge, and `convex-prism` already exists as a collision kind — what does not exist is a renderer that draws one, because `geometry.ts` skips every convex prism on the grounds that an arc's visible body is its own analytic feature. That is the next piece of work and it is a representation change.

`parts:check` is therefore still at 16 and is **not** wired into `verify`, because it exits 1 and wiring it would put `verify` red on `main`.

## I wrote "feasible to fix" into three documents and then measured it false

**Action:** Claimed, in the plan, the devlog and a pushed commit message, that hollow collision for the eight mesh parts was feasible because their winding is consistent. Then probed a column that would actually test it.

**Result:** The winding claim is true and the conclusion does not follow. Down a column 7 LDU from a tube axis on `plate-3x3-corner-round` — in the annulus, between bore and outer wall — the mesh has one up-facing surface and two down-facing ones: the top face at y -4, the cavity ceiling at y 0, and the tube's ring at y 4. There is no up-facing surface at the tube's top, because LDraw places the tube through the ceiling without CSG and the ceiling face spans the cavity underneath it. An unpaired close cannot say where its run began: read one way the tube vanishes, read the other the bore fills. Columns at 0, 5 and 9 LDU from the axis all pair cleanly, which is why the probe had to be aimed at the one place it would not.

**Reasoning:** The evidence for "feasible" was a *necessary* condition dressed up as a sufficient one. Consistent winding removes the obstacle that stops you telling a ceiling from a floor; it says nothing about whether every run has both ends, and in a format with no CSG it does not.

**What it costs and what it buys:** the claim reached a pushed commit message before it was checked, so the correction lands as its own commit rather than as a quiet edit. What it buys is the sharper statement: this repository already knows that an LDraw part has no inside, so crossing parity fails; it now also knows there is no CSG, so orientation pairing fails, and for the same underlying reason.

**Also established, and useful:** `emit-measured-part-tables.py` was run against the pinned archives and reproduces all three generated tables byte for byte after `prettier`. The pipeline is reproducible, so whatever eventually derives hollow collision will show up as exactly its own diff.
