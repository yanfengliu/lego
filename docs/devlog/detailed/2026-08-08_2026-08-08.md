## The stud lattice's frame in Builder units is `diag(25,-25,-25)`, and the Shell mesh is x-mirrored relative to the XML

**Timestamp:** 2026-08-08 09:05

**Action:** Reconciled two conventions that look contradictory. `extract-builder-shell.py` writes `verticesLdu = [-25*v[0], -25*v[1], -25*v[2]]` from the Unity `Shell` mesh, while a field node at XML coordinates `(x,y,z)` must land at `(25x, -25y, -25z)` to reproduce the pinned centres.

**Result:** Both are the same map. The Unity mesh is stored x-negated relative to the XML's own AABB — checked across all fourteen admitted designs, the mesh `min/max` x is exactly the negation of the `Bounding` AABB's — so `-25 * mesh = diag(25,-25,-25) * xml`. That is the `diag(25,-25,-25)` the repository already declares.

**Reasoning:** Worth writing down because the two files disagree on their face and a reader who trusts one and applies it to the other gets a mirrored lattice, which is the exact class of bug that cost this project a day in the `resolveBuilderBoneTransform` reflection.

## The half-turn is outside the family the fit sweeps

**Timestamp:** 2026-08-08 15:25-15:35

**Action:** Swept the settled prefix over four quarter turns, both elevation signs and both image rolls against each panel's built art, with the highlight excluded from both sides.

**Result:** Steps 2 and 3 pick `roll 0, turn 0` — the code's own choice — at 0.9031 and 0.8898 against runners-up of 0.6946 and 0.8276. Step 4 picks `roll 180, turn 90` at 0.8312; the best pose without the roll is 0.7599 and the code's own was 0.4455.

**Reasoning:** Photographing a model that has been turned over is the same as photographing the unturned one through the inverse of the turn, and a half-turn about any horizontal axis carries the up vector to `-Y`. `viewForPanelFace` negated the azimuth and the elevation and left the image upright, so it produced the panel's picture rotated half a turn. That is not reachable by moving the eye: with up pinned to `+Y` the model's own up axis projects above the view axis at every azimuth and every elevation short of straight down, which is now asserted as a sweep in both `camera-fit.test.ts` and `panel-face.test.ts`. It is why the previous session's sweep of sixteen quarter-turn and sign variants was flat at 0.400-0.444 — the answer was never in that family.

The quarter turn is a second, separate unknown. A quarter turn permutes the projected lattice basis (`a(A+90) = b(A)`, `b(A+90) = -a(A)`) and spans the same lattice, so no fit can pin it; the world frame pins it only through the branch printed step 1 settled into, and every rotate-the-model icon reopens it, because the axis the booklet turns about is not stated and lands in that same coset.
