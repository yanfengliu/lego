**Result:** Exact reproduction of the reviewed pins with nothing copied across: `3034;J` gave bundle `0f04e02b…`, primitive XML `05f84da0…`, shell path id `3859792364607955227`, canonical `15d430ab…`, `serializedBytes` 3264 / `vertexDataBytes` 2688 / `indexBufferBytes` 168, an LDraw expansion of exactly 1468 triangles, and a `builderStudCentersLdu` list identical element for element to `real-build-builder-sources.ts`. `3032;F` reproduced both of its geometry slice digests byte for byte — `sha256:21bbef26…` and `sha256:b344f62b…` — which is the encoder, not just the reader, agreeing with `generate-builder-calibration.py`.

## The stud lattice's frame in Builder units is `diag(25,-25,-25)`, and the Shell mesh is x-mirrored relative to the XML

**Timestamp:** 2026-08-08 09:05

**Action:** Reconciled two conventions that look contradictory. `extract-builder-shell.py` writes `verticesLdu = [-25*v[0], -25*v[1], -25*v[2]]` from the Unity `Shell` mesh, while a field node at XML coordinates `(x,y,z)` must land at `(25x, -25y, -25z)` to reproduce the pinned centres.

**Result:** Both are the same map. The Unity mesh is stored x-negated relative to the XML's own AABB — checked across all fourteen admitted designs, the mesh `min/max` x is exactly the negation of the `Bounding` AABB's — so `-25 * mesh = diag(25,-25,-25) * xml`. That is the `diag(25,-25,-25)` the repository already declares.

**Reasoning:** Worth writing down because the two files disagree on their face and a reader who trusts one and applies it to the other gets a mirrored lattice, which is the exact class of bug that cost this project a day in the `resolveBuilderBoneTransform` reflection.

## Two derivations, computed apart, then compared

**Timestamp:** 2026-08-08 09:10–09:20

**Action:** Leg A: the type-23 field of `3020.xml` is a 9x5 lattice at identity rotation, translation `(-0.4, 0.32, -1.2)`, with eight `0:4:1` male nodes. Through `diag(25,-25,-25)` those are `x ∈ {0,20,40,60}`, `y = -8`, `z ∈ {0,20}`. Put in exact integer correspondence with `builtin:plate-2x4`'s eight stud connectors by `deriveCatalogToBuilderFrames`. Leg B: a blind search over all four upright turns and a 2 LDU translation lattice for the rigid motion putting Builder's own Shell vertices closest to the expanded `parts/3020.dat` surface — no stud lattice anywhere in it.

**Result:** Leg A returns exactly two frames, `[30,-4,10] yaw-90` and `[30,-4,10] yaw-270`, whose residual is `{[0,0,0], yaw-180}` — a pure half turn about the part's own origin, which `isCatalogPartSelfSymmetry` accepts, so one equivalence class and the surface is never asked to choose. Leg B's global optimum over the 54 admissible frames is `[-30,8,-10] yaw-0` with its half-turn twin `[30,8,10] yaw-180`, mean 0.3245 LDU, max 1.3056 LDU, with the nearest non-symmetric alternative 2.39x worse. The Builder-to-LDraw motion predicted by leg A composed with the LDraw-side stud correspondence — the two authored lattices, no surfaces — is `[-30,8,-10] yaw-0`. **The two agree exactly**, and `createBuilderFrameEvidence` reports p95 = max = 1.305568 LDU, the same number leg B found as its optimum.

**Reasoning:** `ldrawToCatalogLocalTransform` was derived rather than chosen: `3020.dat`'s own eight `stud.dat` placements at `x ∈ {-30,-10,10,30}`, `y=0`, `z ∈ {-10,10}` were put in the same exact correspondence with the catalog connectors, giving `[0,-4,0]` at a quarter turn. `yaw-0` and `yaw-180` are rejected by the surface at 20.000001 LDU; `yaw-90` and `yaw-270` differ by the plate's own half turn and give byte-identical evidence, so the pin records `yaw-90` for consistency with every other rectangular plate row and the choice is not observable.

## The 9.11px was the loser's number

**Timestamp:** 2026-08-08 19:50–21:30

**Action:** Reproduced printed step 4's `camera-fit-failed` by dumping every candidate `fitStudLattice` considers — basis, explained peaks, unit-cell area, harmonic coherence, axonometric residual and points per stud — for the panels around it, and looked at the panel raster and the fitted overlay rather than only at the numbers.

**Result:** The handed-over lead was that step 4 is the booklet's first underside panel and that an upright-axonometric fit was therefore the wrong family of views. **It is not what refused the panel, and no face effect could have.** The panel's own grid was in the candidate list all along, second, at `a`=(36,14) `b`=(-25,21), 43.83px per stud, **0.474% of pitch** — four times inside the 0.02 gate that refused it. The sort now consults the residual gate where the candidate is chosen and not only after — same threshold, same value, ranked above coarseness and below explained peaks. Over the same forty panels, before and after, **exactly one changes**: step 4 goes from refused to azimuth **34.71**, elevation **35.01**, 43.77px per stud, residual **0.208px = 0.475% of pitch**, coherence 0.181, **16.134 points per stud** against 16.193 and 16.014 either side of it. # Unit — the deferral's second trigger, and what printed step 5 cannot say

Separate session, later the same day, on top of the camera-fit repair recorded above. Subject: printed step 4 refusing with `ambiguous-placement-score`, and whether the one-step lookahead is the right remedy for it.

## The two tied placements are not what the handover said they were

**Action:** Reproduced the refusal at `--last-step 4`, then drove a scratch probe (`zzz-step4-look.spec.ts`, since deleted; source kept out of the repo) that rebuilds the settled three-step prefix from the sealed run's `document.json`, re-derives panel 4 and panel 5 through `derivePanelRasterEvidence`, and scores every distinct placement of `builtin:plate-1x8` through the run's own `createStepSilhouette` and `anchorStepCamera`.

**Result:** 240 distinct placements. Top two: `[60,-8,-50] upright-yaw-270` at `0.2734538947219428` and `[80,0,-50] upright-yaw-270` at `0.2723736926391197`, margin `0.0010802020828231118`. Third is `0.2607786421`. The wedge plate is not ambiguous on either branch: `[60,8,-20] upright-yaw-0` at `0.36635`.

**Reasoning:** The two differ by `[+20,+8,0]` — one stud across the plate and one plate-height down — with the same z and the same yaw. The handover's account ("a long thin plate slid along its own axis barely changes its silhouette") describes a displacement along the plate's long axis, which for `upright-yaw-270` is z, and z is exactly what these two share. What they are is an axonometric degeneracy of *this* camera: one stud sideways and one plate down project to almost the same screen offset. The remedy is the same; the reason it was needed is not what was written down.

## Carrying the whole product forward is not available to a step that has a panel

**Action:** Extended `deferring` with a second trigger (`ownPanelCannotSeparate`) and ran `--last-step 5`.

**Result:** `resource-budget-exhausted` — `240 x 334` whole-step candidates against the explicit 512 budget.

**Reasoning:** Step 1's deferral carries the full product because its panel says nothing at all. Step 4's panel ranks 240 candidates and puts two at the top; it has separated the other 238 from the best by more than `minimumScoreMargin`. `placementsOwnPanelCannotSeparate` applies `selectUniquePlacementScore`'s own rule down the ranking rather than across the top two, which introduces no number: 240 → **2** on the plate, 334 → **1** on the wedge. What it can still get wrong is dropping the drawn placement, and the gate that catches that is the winner's *absolute* agreement, not its margin — which is why the calibration put the discriminator there.

## Panel 5's highlight does not close, so it cannot say what step 5 added

**Action:** Rendered both whole-step candidates and the bare prefix at panel 5's face-corrected camera, registered each against panel 5's already-built art, and looked at the overlays.

**Result:** Agreements `0.529091` and `0.525042` against the 0.85 bar, margin `0.004`. **Reasoning:** The overlay shows it immediately: the long plate and the green 2 x 4 that printed step 5 *places* are still inside the red already-built mask, because only their outline was removed. Filling the contour was not attempted: `extractHighlightRegions` is where that would belong, and swapping the agreement metric for a containment measure is excluded because the 0.85 bar is calibrated on the excluded-IoU quantity and the exclusion's necessity is itself measured (including it inverts the panel-3 ranking).

## Panel 4's registration is flat across every view, which is upstream of everything above

**Action:** Rendered the settled prefix at all sixteen quarter-turn and sign variants of panel 4's fitted view and registered each against panel 4's already-built art.

**Result:** `anchorIou` spans **0.400 to 0.444** — best at azimuth 235.285, elevation -35.014; the shipped view (-34.715, -35.014) sits at 0.4079, and the run's own anchoring reports 0.393. Steps 2 and 3 report 0.903 and 0.627.

**Reasoning:** Step 4's highlight is a closed contour running diagonally across the whole assembly, so `alreadyBuiltMask` cuts a band out of the middle of the very art the registration has to use; a solid prefix silhouette cannot match a fan with a band removed at any offset. That is enough to explain the flatness without a camera error, and it is also enough to make every step-4 score — the 0.0011 included — a number measured through a registration that does not line up. Left open and not guessed at: the anchor could be given the highlight's own region as a don't-care, which is the same idea as the deferral's exclusion applied one panel earlier.
