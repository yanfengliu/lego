**Result:** Exact reproduction of the reviewed pins with nothing copied across: `3034;J` gave bundle `0f04e02b…`, primitive XML `05f84da0…`, shell path id `3859792364607955227`, canonical `15d430ab…`, `serializedBytes` 3264 / `vertexDataBytes` 2688 / `indexBufferBytes` 168, an LDraw expansion of exactly 1468 triangles, and a `builderStudCentersLdu` list identical element for element to `real-build-builder-sources.ts`. `3032;F` reproduced both of its geometry slice digests byte for byte — `sha256:21bbef26…` and `sha256:b344f62b…` — which is the encoder, not just the reader, agreeing with `generate-builder-calibration.py`.

## The stud lattice's frame in Builder units is `diag(25,-25,-25)`, and the Shell mesh is x-mirrored relative to the XML

**Timestamp:** 2026-08-08 09:05

**Action:** Reconciled two conventions that look contradictory. `extract-builder-shell.py` writes `verticesLdu = [-25*v[0], -25*v[1], -25*v[2]]` from the Unity `Shell` mesh, while a field node at XML coordinates `(x,y,z)` must land at `(25x, -25y, -25z)` to reproduce the pinned centres.

**Result:** Both are the same map. The Unity mesh is stored x-negated relative to the XML's own AABB — checked across all fourteen admitted designs, the mesh `min/max` x is exactly the negation of the `Bounding` AABB's — so `-25 * mesh = diag(25,-25,-25) * xml`. That is the `diag(25,-25,-25)` the repository already declares.

**Reasoning:** Worth writing down because the two files disagree on their face and a reader who trusts one and applies it to the other gets a mirrored lattice, which is the exact class of bug that cost this project a day in the `resolveBuilderBoneTransform` reflection.

## Two derivations, computed apart, then compared

**Timestamp:** 2026-08-08 09:10–09:20

**Action:** Leg A: the type-23 field of `3020.xml` is a 9x5 lattice at identity rotation, translation `(-0.4, 0.32, -1.2)`, with eight `0:4:1` male nodes. Through `diag(25,-25,-25)` those are `x ∈ {0,20,40,60}`, `y = -8`, `z ∈ {0,20}`. Put in exact integer correspondence with `builtin:plate-2x4`'s eight stud connectors by `deriveCatalogToBuilderFrames`. Leg B: a blind search over all four upright turns and a 2 LDU translation lattice for the rigid motion putting Builder's own Shell vertices closest to the expanded `parts/3020.dat` surface — no stud lattice anywhere in it.

**Result:** Leg A returns exactly two frames, `[30,-4,10] yaw-90` and `[30,-4,10] yaw-270`, whose residual is `{[0,0,0], yaw-180}` — a pure half turn about the part's own origin, which `isCatalogPartSelfSymmetry` accepts, so one equivalence class and the surface is never asked to choose. Leg B's global optimum over the 54 admissible frames is `[-30,8,-10] yaw-0` with its half-turn twin `[30,8,10] yaw-180`, mean 0.3245 LDU, max 1.3056 LDU, with the nearest non-symmetric alternative 2.39x worse. The Builder-to-LDraw motion predicted by leg A composed with the LDraw-side stud correspondence — the two authored lattices, no surfaces — is `[-30,8,-10] yaw-0`. **The two agree exactly**, and `createBuilderFrameEvidence` reports p95 = max = 1.305568 LDU, the same number leg B found as its optimum.

**Reasoning:** `ldrawToCatalogLocalTransform` was derived rather than chosen: `3020.dat`'s own eight `stud.dat` placements at `x ∈ {-30,-10,10,30}`, `y=0`, `z ∈ {-10,10}` were put in the same exact correspondence with the catalog connectors, giving `[0,-4,0]` at a quarter turn. `yaw-0` and `yaw-180` are rejected by the surface at 20.000001 LDU; `yaw-90` and `yaw-270` differ by the plate's own half turn and give byte-identical evidence, so the pin records `yaw-90` for consistency with every other rectangular plate row and the choice is not observable.
