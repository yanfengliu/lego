## Offline rig, and the divergence it introduced

**Timestamp:** 2026-08-08 19:50

**Action:** Dumped the forty camera-fit panel crops and their include masks to `output/zzz-crops` from a scratch Playwright probe, converted them to raw RGBA with PIL, and re-ran `fitStudLattice` on them in Node.

**Result:** Reproduces the browser harness on **40 of 40** panels — same accept/refuse, same points per stud to 0.02 — at under a second per panel. That turned a ten-minute browser round trip into a two-minute sweep and made a 1698-rule search affordable.

**What it cost, and the lesson:** the rig reimplemented candidate enumeration on top of the real primitives, and its order-free pairing was written as "put each vector below the horizon, then read the roles off the sign of `x`". That is subtly different from the real fix, because a vector with `y = 0` is the same repeat under either sign. The rig therefore predicted printed step 38 would stay refused and the real code accepted it at 1.12 points per stud. **A rig that reimplements the thing it is measuring will diverge from it, and the divergence will be the interesting case.** Resolved by copying the patched module into the scratchpad with the two switches exposed, so every counterfactual below is the production code with one flag flipped.

## The fix is two changes and neither works alone

**Timestamp:** 2026-08-08 20:29

**Action:** Ran the production `fitStudLattice` over all forty panels with `orderFree` and `strongestPeak` independently on and off.

**Result:**

| order-free pairing | strongest-peak key | fitted | at booklet scale | wrongly accepted | step 4 | step 7 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| no | no | 33 | 33 | none | fit 16.13 | **REFUSED 21.72** |
| no | yes | 33 | 33 | none | fit 16.13 | **REFUSED 21.72** |
| yes | no | 35 | 33 | 32@21.00, 38@7.43 | **REFUSED 10.34** | fit 15.95 |
| yes | yes | 35 | **34** | 32@21.00 | fit 16.13 | fit 15.95 |

**Reasoning:** The strongest-peak key is *inert* under the old pairing — identical fitted set, identical refusals — and that is the point. It exists to survive the pairs the order-free pairing admits. The previous session's note that "the two changes have to be measured together" was right, and the missing third thing was a key that neither of them is.

**What the strongest-peak key says:** a lattice with no site at the picture's strongest autocorrelation repeat is not a reading of that picture. It has no constant in it. It is needed because the explained-peak *count* is monotone under refinement — a finer lattice has a site wherever the coarse one does — so the count can never refuse a lattice built on a drawing's sub-pitch repeats, and both problem panels are underside views whose plate ribbing draws exactly those.

## Not done, and deliberately

It no longer bites at printed step 7, and it will bite again at the first deferral whose settling panel is one of the five still refused. Left out of this change so the loop's movement is attributable to one thing.
