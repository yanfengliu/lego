## Outcome and first increment

Build one bounded inverse experiment for a declared family of opaque upright circular studs on an opaque rectangular platform, using an observed graph of raster boundary chains. First recover the graph without geometric ownership labels. Then enumerate its possible physical incidence assignments and enclose the associated signed no-roll orthographic cameras. No rendered camera candidates are generated or scored. The first positive requires at least one fully supported cap-rim cycle, a supported cap/wall/base incidence, and two nonparallel supported platform edge directions. Open or interrupted chains remain in the graph; this increment does not pretend that any short arc determines an ellipse.

The first experiment uses eight newly authored synthetic RGBA observations and an independently checked physical interpretation of their visible geometry. It does not read the retained catalog PNGs. It establishes only whether this particular observation mechanism handles its declared positive, ambiguity, and contradiction controls. Native catalog transfer, physical placement, production acceptance, protected-source access, and first-50 completion remain outside scope.

Preparation is complete; executable implementation is deliberately not included. The exact new source bytes, their source audit, and their hashes must exist and be admitted before the proposed command can run. The named unresolved work is implementing the bounded chain tracer and conservative interval contractor below, then independently checking their containment and graph coverage. A declarative constraint list alone is not a working inverse solver.

## Assumptions and exact limits of the physical claim

The declared synthetic physical family has a horizontal opaque rectangular platform, four disjoint upright convex circular studs, radius 6 LDU, stud height 4 LDU, platform height 8 LDU and pitch 20 LDU. Those scalar dimensions are source-derived from `packages/catalog/src/constants.ts`; they are premises of this synthetic family, not recovered part identities. No global document frame or part-instance identifier is supplied to the extractor.

The image coordinate system has origin at the top left, x right and y down. Image reflection is absent and `upSign` is positive one. These are fixed experimental conditions, not quantities inferred from contour appearance. Physical y is up in the equations; catalog negative-y-up is a separate representation conversion. Camera elevation is restricted to the nondegenerate range strictly between -90 and 90 degrees, with positive image scale. If those image conventions are not known, the corresponding sign/reflection branches must be added before solving.

The coordinator identified a separate campaign rotation, and its pure source was checked: `proper-m-00nn000p0` in `packages/catalog/src/proper-orientations.generated.ts` has row-major matrix `[0,0,-1; -1,0,0; 0,1,0]`. Rendering converts catalog coordinates with `C = diag(1,-1,1)/20`. Thus local stud-top-minus-base `-Y` maps to world `-Z`, while local row `+Z` maps to world `-X`. This first experiment does not include that rotated subject. Its horizontal-rim ellipse and image-vertical wall equations are not valid there. `upSign` remains a camera/image convention; it cannot be inferred from or substituted for that horizontal-world stud-axis displacement. A separately admitted rotated-axis extension is required before campaign-native qualification.

Physical region colors are independent nuisance values; no cap-brighter-than-wall or background-brightness order is used. Surfaces are opaque and untextured, and ink belongs to actual visible surface boundaries. The initial raster policy declares its finite sampling and ink width before execution. Arbitrary printed texture is excluded as a premise, because a flat sheet bearing the exact cylinder drawing gives identical RGBA to the raised subject: no inverse-image method can distinguish those two inputs without a physical prior. That is a constructive restricted identifiability gap, not permission to stop this strategy.

The first positive does not use an added fiducial, encoded marker, supplied stud index, or a prelabelled world-axis direction. The observed platform corner and two physical edge direction classes constrain a horizontal orthogonal frame. Both assignments of those classes to physical x/z and both directed signs remain live unless a checked symmetry of the complete physical subject proves that two encodings are the same. For the symmetric fixture, a union of narrow camera branches is the intended positive result; a unique yaw is not required or allowed by a tie-break.

## Observed graph before interpretation

Each input is native 720 by 470 top-down RGBA. The tracer first records the neighboring pixel pair behind every boundary sample. For every horizontal or vertical neighbor pair, its contrast is the maximum absolute channel difference, including alpha. Trace the seven preregistered contrast levels `0, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64`, using strict contrast greater than the level. These are diagnostic observation levels, not new production tolerances. The experiment retains every level and its differences; it does not select the level with the best later camera answer.

An edge sample is the shared side between two pixel cells, together with both original pixel indices and their RGBA values. Connect those dual-grid segments into maximal chains between endpoints or junctions. Closed components keep their full ordered cycle. Four-way junctions retain their alternative continuations rather than silently choosing one. Chain reversal, cycle start position, and graph-ID renaming can be canonicalized because they preserve the same pixel support and incidence; joining a gap, erasing a branch, swapping physical rims, changing an occluder, or exchanging physical axes cannot.

The initial localization envelope is the declared sampling/ink envelope of each synthetic observation. Pixel uncertainty is kept as a spatial interval around each sample, never converted into a best-fit scalar residual. A future 24-sided native stud has an additional circular support error bounded by `0.009 * projectedRadius`, since `1 - cos(pi/24) < 0.009`; this extra term is not needed for the initial analytic fixture and cannot be asserted for a retained historical render without separately closing its actual geometry. No native image is used to tune an envelope here.

The graph output contains observed chain support, endpoints/junctions, contrast alternatives, adjacency-side samples, and unknown intervals. It contains no ellipse center, physical stud index, top/base label, camera, or confidence label. Empty and open graphs are valid observations. Caps, base rims, centers, wall planes, and platform ownership appear only in the subsequent physical-hypothesis output.

## Joint graph and camera interpretation

Use the observed cycles and junctions to enumerate a small grammar: a supported cap rim, two observed wall generators, a supported visible base arc, and the visible surrounding platform face. A complete cap cycle can be split by its wall junctions without replacing the original support. A base rim is normally only a visible arc; its center and invisible arc are latent variables, not observed circle samples. An occluded cap arc remains missing and does not become a complete observed rim because another stud shares its dimensions.

The first increment requires at least one complete rim cycle to bound horizontal and vertical extrema. For a claimed full projected circle, every permitted center and semiaxis lies within the observed extrema intervals, enlarged only by the declared spatial envelope. The contractor checks every assigned boundary segment against the conic, and checks a complete cycle's winding and quadrant coverage. A rectangle with the same extrema must fail its intermediate segment checks. Partial rim chains may constrain a shared camera and a particular physical assignment, but cannot supply nonexistent extrema.

For each grammar assignment, intersect the conic, common-radius, common-height, vertical-generator, incidence, visible-half, platform-direction and row-pitch constraints together. Assigning a cap independently and then fitting any camera around it is forbidden. Repeated rows must use distinct observed support; the same cap cannot be counted twice. If an extra physical stud is observed, retain that count rather than choosing four convenient members. Missing support remains partial. A candidate cannot hide an unexplained strong boundary by assigning it an unrestricted noise or texture label; the observation either lies inside the declared sampling/ink envelope of a physical boundary, names an actual occluder, or makes that physical hypothesis unsupported.

The positive sign of elevation may be restricted only by a physical incidence explanation: the complete outward upper cap is visible, its wall generators meet the base/front arc, and that base belongs on the visible opaque platform top under the convex upright-stud hypothesis. Brightness order does not establish any of those meanings. If the supporting-face incidence or cap visibility is missing, preserve alternate ownership/sign explanations or return unsupported incidence. Report this as conditional geometry evidence, never functional-connector or material authority.

## Camera equations and conservative representation

Let `s > 0` be pixels per LDU, `c = cos(azimuth)`, `t = sin(azimuth)`, `q = sin(elevation)`, and `k = cos(elevation) > 0`. The no-roll projection in the declared image convention is:

```text
u = tx + s * (c*x - t*z)
v = ty + s * (q*t*x - k*y + q*c*z)
c*c + t*t = 1
q*q + k*k = 1
```

A horizontal circular stud rim has horizontal semiaxis `a = 6*s` and vertical semiaxis `b = a*abs(q)`. The base-center minus top-center vertical displacement is `d = 4*s*k`. Centers are latent even when the entire rim is supported. The jointly required identity is `(b/a)^2 + (6*d/(4*a))^2 = 1`. This cross-check must reject a shared ellipse family whose wall height cannot belong to the same camera. It never substitutes a visible ink edge for a measured center without its interval derivation.

The projected horizontal axis directions are proportional to `(c, q*t)` and `(-t, q*c)`. Where neither direction is vertical, slopes satisfy `mX*mZ = -q*q`; vertical directions are handled by the original vector equations instead of dividing by zero. This relates the independently observed platform edge directions to the ellipse/wall measurement and rejects a rhombus chosen only because it looks grid-like. Each axis/sign correspondence remains a separate physical branch.

Represent camera output as a finite collection of rational interval boxes over `s,c,t,q,k,tx,ty`, together with the surviving physical correspondence and the correlated equations above. Use conservative interval arithmetic and exact rational endpoints for pruning; a box is deleted only when an obligation is impossible over the entire box. Do not convert an unproved box midpoint into a feasible camera. The result is a conservative outer enclosure, not a guarantee that every enclosed tuple is attainable. A point-valued display angle may be supplied only as an explicitly nonauthoritative illustration, and is unnecessary in the first increment.

Derive initial scale and elevation intervals from full-rim extrema and wall displacement. Derive the azimuth branches from observed platform direction intervals. Bisect only unresolved image-feature/camera intervals, retaining all children that cannot be disproved. Splitting order is a deterministic work order and has no selection authority. At budget exhaustion, emit `unresolved-budget` with every remaining box and its active constraints; never call surviving unknown boxes a positive. At a minimum, each accepted conditional branch must have the expected signed elevation, exclude the opposite sign by its incidence constraints, enclose an independently established witness, and show strict contraction of its angle intervals from the initial quadrant/domain. Region widths and count are reported without a tuned single-angle cutoff.

## Eight fixed synthetic observations

Author the positive in image space using rational geometry. Platform top corners are `(200,104)`, `(520,248)`, `(460,296)`, `(140,152)`. Its two visible side faces extend 32 pixels down. Four cap centers are `(210,130) + i*(80,36)` for `i = 0,1,2,3`, with semiaxes `30,18`. Each visible front base arc is centered 16 pixels below its cap center, and the two wall generators connect the horizontal tangency points. The cap is a full ellipse; only the appropriate front base half is drawn. Occlusion and the filled supporting face, not color naming, make those incidences visible. The source family has radius 6, height 4, pitch 20 and platform height 8; an independent checker can verify the rational witness `s=5, c=4/5, t=3/5, q=3/5, k=4/5` directly from the coordinates. The extractor never receives that witness, stud indices, physical labels, or palette-role labels.

The synthetic fixture author must separately validate that its painter/occlusion ordering actually produces this opaque geometry. A drawing whose hidden lines are accidentally shown does not establish a positive, even if the solver and generator agree about it. The fixture is intentionally simple enough for native image inspection and an independent coordinate audit. Its base case is not a claim about the retained catalog renderer.

1. **P0, supported geometry:** the complete subject above. Require supported cap/wall/base correspondences, the conservative camera union, inclusion of the independent rational witness, and retention of all unresolved x/z/sign assignments.
2. **P1, photometric control:** form the observation by bytewise RGB inversion of P0's final decoded raster, keeping alpha unchanged. This exactly preserves every adjacent-channel absolute difference, independent of raster rounding. Require the same raw boundary incidence at every contrast level and the same physical/camera union. This falsifies a hidden brightness-order classifier.
3. **P2, partial visibility:** place one visible opaque foreground polygon over the upper quarter of one cap and its adjacent wall. Require the occluded chain to stay open, that stud's unobserved claims to stay latent/partial, and the other supported studs to constrain a camera. It must not report four completely observed rims.
4. **A0, cap-only ambiguity:** remove all platform/wall/base drawing, retaining only the cap ellipses. Require no raised-stud or signed-face conclusion. Report precisely the remaining ellipse/row constraints and missing incidence.
5. **A1, single-cylinder ambiguity:** retain one complete cylinder silhouette/rims but remove the platform and other studs. All azimuths give the same contour graph under free region colors, because rotating a vertical circular cylinder around its own axis changes no surface. Require a continuous azimuth ambiguity, not a chosen yaw.
6. **N0, no-stud control:** platform only. Require zero supported cylinder triples and no stud-derived camera claim.
7. **N1, inconsistent height:** in P0 move one front base arc and its two wall endpoints eight pixels down, keeping its cap and the other studs fixed. Require rejection of a single common-height/common-camera four-stud assignment. Three supported studs may remain as a partial hypothesis; that is not a complete pass.
8. **N2, false conic:** in P0 replace one complete cap ellipse by a rectangle with the same extrema while retaining its wall/base boundaries. Require the conic segment constraints to reject the four-stud assignment; an extrema-only implementation must fail this control.

There is also a zero-extra-raster impossibility witness: reinterpret P0's exact RGBA as a texture printed on a flat opaque sheet. Its pixels and observed graph are identical. The program must not claim that pixels alone refute this alternative. The physical result is conditional on the explicit untextured-geometry premise.

The component that records expected labels/witnesses is separate from the extractor. It is read only after all eight immutable extraction outputs have been written. Independently inspect every native fixture and every graph overlay, with exact PNG and decoded-RGBA hashes bound to that review. A collage does not replace those eight individual inspections.

## Fixed measurements and disqualifiers

Record per input: original RGBA digest; full observed-edge and chain counts at each contrast level; uncertain junction count; every complete and partial ownership assignment; observed versus latent role support; unexplained boundary count; proposed and pruned camera boxes; retained physical alternatives; camera interval widths; reason for every refusal; memory/work counters; and completion versus budget exhaustion.

The control evaluator records `witnessExcluded`, `unsupportedCompleteClaim`, `lostInequivalentAlternative`, `negativeFourStudAssignment`, and `budgetExhausted`, each against its stated applicable denominator. All must be zero for the declared initial acceptance conditions. P0 must produce a supported conditional camera region and P1 must preserve it; P2 must retain the explicit missing-support facts; A0/A1 must retain the stipulated ambiguity; N0/N1/N2 must lack the prohibited complete assignment. No population of automatically refused inputs counts as a positive experiment. A wide unconstrained region, an unimplemented constraint system, or an output created after truncating alternatives is incomplete.

Specific disqualifiers: treating a latent conic center as a detected pixel; closing an occlusion gap; top/base assignment by intensity; selecting one threshold/correspondence/camera because it scores best; dropping an axis assignment without a complete-subject symmetry proof; using a generator label as an observed direction; replacing failed rows or widening their raster bounds after seeing them; fitting only extrema; claiming circle accuracy for faceted native geometry without a shape envelope; or calling a conservative possible box an actually feasible camera.

## Resource bound and proposed implementation closure

Eight images, 720 by 470 each. One headless Node process, no browser or subprocess. Input synthetic RGBA is generated locally from a frozen source; no repository sample, image, PDF, or model input is read. Maximum 65,536 retained boundary segments and 1,024 chains per image across the compact contrast representation, 256 material role assignments per image, and 262,144 interval subdivision nodes per image. Crossing any limit returns an explicit incomplete result with remaining alternatives; no top-k fallback is permitted. Maximum resident working allocation is 256 MiB, total output is 64 MiB, and each image has a 60-second work deadline; failure is not success. These are experimental work ceilings, not production policy changes.

Only five proposed new source files are needed, each under 500 lines: `fixture.mjs` (direct synthetic 2D geometry/raster and separately withheld expected facts), `trace.mjs` (pixel-pair graph), `inverse.mjs` (grammar and camera contractor), `interval.mjs` (bounded rational interval operations), and `run.mjs` (fixed command, counters, immutable results). There is no catalog/rendering import, no evaluator/promotion call, no dependency addition, no runtime loader helper, no filesystem discovery, and no source edit.
