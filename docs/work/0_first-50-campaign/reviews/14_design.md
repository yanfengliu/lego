### Focused independent F9/F10 assessment

#### F10 is resolved at design level

Addendum lines 9-13 remove `tx,ty` from the identified-camera interval tuple and explicitly make a supported observed vertex the local coordinate origin. The raw support IDs and uncertainty box remain attached. The deterministic anchor ordering chooses a gauge, without choosing a physical owner or a signed axis. Relative translation is named as such, and conversion between different anchors does not erase physical alternatives or claim a canonical document frame. This removes the unbounded translation gauge from the reported identified parameters and addresses F10 directly.

The illustrative anchor `(140,152)` is the minimum-image-x outer platform vertex of P0. In the audit's original axes, changing origin there gives the stated platform range `x in [0,80], z in [-20,0]` and stud bases `(10+20*i,0,-10)`. That vertex is outside the studs and the fixed P2 foreground polygon. In fact all four outer platform vertices are outside that polygon. These are coordinate/geometry checks, not observations that the future extractor has successfully found a vertex. The requirement that an actual supported vertex be identified, with an honest uncertainty box, remains necessary for a diagnostic relative projection. Its failure no longer forces a false bounded global translation claim.

#### Fixed sampling and direct support are clearer

Pixel-center sampling, no antialiasing, zero ink width, an explicit palette, and a single support identity with seven membership bits make the initial graph contract much more concrete. Every distinct palette pair differs by more than 64 in at least one RGB channel, so the declared un-antialiased fixture has the same unequal-color neighbor support at all seven thresholds. RGB inversion preserves those support identities, memberships and incidences exactly; the side RGB values change. This statement does not certify the unbuilt tracer or painter.

The two-direction support obligation in line 23 is essential. An assigned raw sample must be near the physical boundary, and a boundary portion claimed fully observed must have nearby raw support. Full extrema must satisfy this obligation separately. It is not enough to fit a conic near several samples and claim that its unseen extremes were measured. P2's fixed polygon removes upper/left cap support and the left generator of the first stud; the three other studs and the outer platform vertices remain outside it. The parent prohibition against closing that occlusion gap remains appropriate.

For a complete rim whose audited extremum is within Chebyshev distance 2 of raw support, and whose matching candidate extremum is independently within 2 of the corresponding support, candidate extrema are within 4 per coordinate of the audit. Thus `a in [26,34]` and `b in [14,22]` give exactly `s in [13/3,17/3]` and `abs(q) in [7/17,11/13]`. The positive sign still requires supported physical cap/wall/base/platform incidence. These fractions are correct conditional bounds. This review does not treat a generic sample-to-conic distance as a proof of the required complete-extrema correspondence, and the addendum correctly retains independent support checks before a positive run can pass.

#### A narrow alternative yaw condition is available for a successor

The following is proposed algebra, not an executed inference or a repair contained in the reviewed addendum. It can avoid locating candidate vertices by using well-separated points on one directly supported straight edge. Suppose two audited points on that same physical edge each have raw support within 2 pixels, and the candidate line matches those exact assigned supports within 2 pixels. There then exist two points on the candidate line within 4 pixels per coordinate of the audited points. Their displacement differs from the audited displacement by at most 8 per coordinate. These points need not be candidate vertices, so the line-intersection amplification does not apply. Mere proximity to the union of several boundaries is insufficient; the support must belong to and be matched by the same assigned edge.

For the fully visible front edge from `D=(140,152)` to `C=(460,296)`, this premise yields direction components `du in [312,328]`, `dv in [136,152]`, hence slope magnitude in `[17/41,19/39]`. Combining it with positive `q in [7/17,11/13]` and the no-roll direction equation gives `abs(t/c) in [221/451,323/273]` when that direction is assigned to X, or `[273/323,451/221]` when assigned to Z. These bounds are looser than the pending vertex-based proposal but exclude an almost unchanged quadrant through explicit numbers. They require the same-edge support and matching premise; if support near the two end points is ambiguous, independently justified separated interior points and their actual span must replace them before freezing a bound.

The front edge is a reasonable place to investigate that premise. Its line is `v-0.45*u-89=0`. The maximum value over a stud's base ellipse is `-37.5+22.5=-15`; the cap and wall remain farther from that edge or within the same bound. The P2 polygon's maximum value is `-21.3`. Thus the analytic opaque subject and that polygon do not occlude the front edge. This geometric clearance does not by itself prove the raw chain's sampling coverage, role assignment, or candidate-line matching. A successor must state those exact obligations and must not insert the audited endpoints or camera values as extractor observations. Other directly supported line-direction bounds are possible; this round does not select an implementation.
