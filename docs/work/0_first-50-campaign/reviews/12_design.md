## Reports

### Independent mathematical and observation review

The bounded upright strategy is viable. Its restriction to an analytic untextured opaque subject is explicit. The target does not claim that the equations qualify the campaign's rotated subject or that an inverse implementation already exists. It distinguishes observation support from latent geometry, holds unresolved axis assignments, and rejects a unique yaw obtained through an arbitrary tie-break. The eight observations are a reasonable bounded first population, subject to the material acceptance changes below and the already named future fixture, source, and runtime checks.

#### Physical positive and opaque visibility

The coordinates in proposal line 77 are consistent with a rectangular platform of dimensions 80 by 20 LDU, platform top at physical Y = 0, and camera translation `(200,104)` in a local frame whose X/Z origin is the first stated platform corner. The four stud base centers are `(10 + 20*i, 0, 10)`, with top centers at Y = 4. Under the declared witness `s = 5`, `cos A = 4/5`, `sin A = 3/5`, `sin e = 3/5`, and `cos e = 4/5`, one row pitch projects to `(80,36)`, one platform-width displacement projects to `(-60,48)`, stud height projects to 16 pixels, and platform height projects to 32 pixels. The radius-6 rings project to semiaxes 30 and 18. These are physically consistent positions and dimensions, not merely a rectangular grid decorated with unrelated ellipses.

The painter must nevertheless perform real opaque occlusion. In particular, the rear platform edge from `(200,104)` to `(520,248)` lies on `v - 0.45*u - 14 = 0`. Each cap center has value 21.5 under that functional; each ellipse has support radius `sqrt((0.45*30)^2 + 18^2) = 22.5`. Thus every cap crosses that edge by exactly 1 pixel in the unnormalized line functional. The edge portions behind the cap/stud must be clipped. A painter that draws the entire platform edge over the studs exposes hidden linework and is not the positive promised here. Proposal line 79 correctly makes independent painter and native-image inspection prerequisites. The coordinate audit establishes realizability, not that the future painter meets it.

The full cap cycle, two flank generators, and visible base arc can establish the stated cylinder geometry only when their physical incidences are supported. At finite ink width, adjacent raw pixel chains need not meet at one mathematical rim/generator junction. The implementation must relate their pixel support through the frozen sampling/ink envelope and retain the original support, rather than closing a gap and then treating the repaired graph as observed. The full rim and opaque supporting-face evidence can constrain elevation to the positive branch under this declared family. Brightness rank is not that evidence.

#### Photometric and partial-support controls

For each RGB channel, bytewise inversion obeys `abs((255-a)-(255-b)) = abs(a-b)`. Alpha is unchanged. Therefore the maximum four-channel neighbor contrast and the strict predicates at all seven levels `0,2,4,8,16,32,64` are exactly preserved. P1 can require identical supported dual-grid edges and their topology at every level. Its adjacency-side RGB values are inverted, so the complete raw records need not be byte-identical. A physical interpretation that treats colors as nuisance values must preserve the camera/correspondence union as well. This control catches dependence on brightness polarity; it does not by itself prove independence from every possible palette shortcut.

The implementation must freeze how the seven nested contrast graphs contribute constraints, preserve their support identities, and avoid counting the same pixel-pair evidence at several thresholds as several physical rims. Invariance is required even at a threshold where a boundary disappears. This review does not assume every threshold will independently support a positive camera. The palette, raster sampling, ink width, P2 occluder coordinates, and graph combination rule remain implementation-preflight facts to inspect before execution.

The proposal correctly permits a visible base arc with a latent center and an unobserved continuation. It correctly refuses to fill P2's missing cap support using another stud's dimensions. A partial observed arc can constrain its parent conic when rank and uncertainty permit; an inferred continuation is still not observed. N1 should reject the complete four-stud common-height interpretation while allowing an explicitly incomplete three-stud result. N2 should reject the complete four-stud interpretation through intermediate conic support, not an extrema-only check. A0 and A1 preserve different limitations: absent attachment/face incidence in A0 and continuous azimuth symmetry in A1. None of these eight cases proves general physical identification beyond the declared family.

#### F9: the acceptance predicate does not yet rule out vacuous or unsound enclosures

Proposal lines 71-74 and 96-100 correctly distinguish a conservative outer enclosure from feasible points, but the operative positive requirement is only witness inclusion, expected elevation sign, and strict angle contraction from an initial quadrant/domain. Strict contraction has no minimum useful meaning: replacing an initial quadrant by that quadrant minus an arbitrarily small strip is strict contraction. Such a box can contain the declared witness and remain practically unconstrained. The prose says that a wide unconstrained result is incomplete but supplies no reproducible predicate that distinguishes it from that nearly unchanged box. This leaves a false positive route even for a program that correctly handles the easy negative geometry controls.

Witness inclusion is also one-sided evidence. It shows that this witness was not lost. It does not show that other feasible cameras or inequivalent physical assignments survived. A contractor could retain a narrow region around the witness and incorrectly prune the rest while passing that check. `lostInequivalentAlternative` names the desired failure counter but does not yet define an independently established population or a method that can detect omitted continuous feasible regions. Merely attaching the correlated equations to broad boxes or reporting all invoked constraints is not a proof that a useful contraction happened or that pruning was safe.

Before implementation freeze and execution admission, the successor should preregister a bounded nonvacuity predicate tied to the frozen observation envelopes and the specific claimed parameters. It need not impose one arbitrary universal angle cutoff. It does need an independently checkable numerical or structural condition that a nearly unchanged quadrant cannot satisfy. Each pruning operation also needs a stated mathematical enclosure obligation and an independent check appropriate to it. Exact rational reference checks and deliberately unsound-prune mutations can test arithmetic and predicate classes; a proof of the enclosing arithmetic/contractors remains distinct from finite witness checks. The correspondence audit must identify the complete discrete alternative population within this fixture grammar and its declared symmetry quotient. Require the checks to reject a near-full-domain output, a witness-only region that discards an independently established feasible alternative, and a solver that reports a negative control correctly while doing no meaningful positive contraction. Do not import the generator's camera answer into the extractor to meet these requirements.

#### F10: finite translation boxes need an explicit local origin gauge

The proposal withholds a global physical frame in line 26 but includes `tx,ty` in finite camera boxes in line 71 without defining a local origin. With projection `p = t + s*M*X`, replacing every physical coordinate by `X + delta` and replacing `t` by `t - s*M*delta` leaves every image point, edge incidence, conic and occlusion relation unchanged. Since the no-roll projection has rank two, the unrestricted image translation parameter spans an unbounded gauge orbit. A finite bounded collection over `tx,ty` cannot enclose all of it unless an origin convention or a finite external prior is added.

The narrow remedy is to choose, for each physical correspondence, a local origin at an actually observed platform vertex, preserve the possible axis assignments, and explicitly report translation relative to that chosen local frame. For example, the coordinate audit above uses the first stated corner, but that is an audit convention and is not currently an extractor contract. The gauge choice is neither a detected canonical document origin nor another physical ambiguity; coordinate encodings of the same physical interpretation should be identified only under the stated gauge equivalence. Alternatively, omit translation from the identified-camera output and report anchored image locations separately. The observation graph must remain free of supplied world labels, and a chosen local frame must not acquire document or placement authority.

#### Upright and rotated claim boundaries

The upright equations in lines 58-69 are correct with the proposal's notation: `q` is sine of elevation and is not `upSign`, which is fixed to positive one. The identity `(b/a)^2 + (6*d/(4*a))^2 = 1` correctly joins rim shape and known stud height. A symmetric rectangular row does not determine unique world-axis labels or directed yaw. The proposal's preserved correspondence branches are necessary.

The known proper rotation is explicitly excluded in line 30, which is appropriate for this first experiment. No rotated implementation is requested by this review. Its future extension must use the transformed cylinder axis. With local row `+Z` mapping to world `-X` and stud top-minus-base mapping to world `-Z`, the normalized observed row and axis displacements obey `L/p = -upSign*s*(cos A, sin e*sin A)` and `T/h = upSign*s*(sin A, -sin e*cos A)`. Their signed determinant can recover sine of elevation when both physical directions are owned, but the pair `(upSign,A,e)` and `(-upSign,A+pi,e)` remains identical for those projection measurements. A displacement in world Z does not directly supply `upSign`; a directed world-Y incidence, authoritative metadata, or independently validated sided visibility must distinguish that pair. The rim ellipse is generally rotated and has minor/major ratio `abs(cos e*cos A)`, not `abs(sin e)`. Image reflection, camera metadata, the catalog coordinate conversion, and a physical improper transform remain separate operations.

#### Resource, closure, and document accuracy

The finite eight-case scope, explicit incomplete outcomes, no top-k fallback, and retained failed runs are appropriate. Limits on segments, chains, role assignments, subdivision nodes, memory, output, and time still need implementation-level enforcement. In particular, a graph budget must not truncate support or alternative continuations and then pass. The exact rational implementation needs bounded intermediate allocation, not merely a limit on final box count. Complete runtime metadata reads, the five new sources, the codec and runtime identity must be pinned before execution as the proposal already requires. These are future acceptance checks, not evidence that the proposed command is currently safe or runnable.

The document is accurate about its unbuilt state. It marks the contractor, tracer, source closure and metadata-read admission as unresolved, separates synthetic success from native transfer and campaign acceptance, and does not represent a source inspection as execution authority. This review grants no source-bearing execution or implementation acceptance.
